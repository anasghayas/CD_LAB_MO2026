Protocol for the Compiler Design Laboratory Course
Read this document and follow them strictly for the entire duration of the laboratory.
1. Assignment problems and accompanying handouts, if any, will be shared with the students a day in advance of
the schedule. You are expected to read the document(s) and come prepared with the requisite skills.
2. Summary of the work to be done during a session, in terms of how, where and what to submit at the close of
the session, will be communicated in the assignment sheet and must be followed.
3. The objective of each session, in terms of the resources needed, the theoretical concepts that are reinforced,
the specific skills that are acquired, are mentioned in the assignment. This information should help a student to
measure her/his learning incrementally as the course proceeds.
4. Programs are to be written in C, compiled with gcc, and executed under the Linux Operating system. You will
learn two powerful software tools, Lex and Yacc as part of this laboratory course.
5. Each student will be assigned separate login on a server. You are required to set a password and remember the
same for the semester. Choosing a password that can not be easily guessed is the responsibility of the student.
Sharing of password is PROHIBITED. Two or more students, if found to access other’s resources (files /
folders), will be held equally responsible and face disciplinary action.
5. You are NOT PERMITTED to access the area of another student for ANY REASON whatsoever. Violation
can be easily detected and will be treated as UFM (Use of Unfair Means) according to institute norms.
6. You are NOT permitted to use internet, either institute’s or your own, during the session. Make sure that your
MOBILES ARE SWITCHED OFF. If there is a need to access msteams, the faculty will inform you about the
way to do so.
7. DO NOT copy programs from any source, either that of your friend or from a s/w such as ChatGPT. This
applies to all programs written by you, whether in C, lex-scripts or yacc-scripts during the day to day
performance or for regular evaluations. Check for plagiarism of programs among students may be deployed by
the faculty team, if the situation demands so.
8. In case you wish to use your own laptop for the practicals, you must take prior approval from your concerned
faculty. Ensure that the laptop is your own with both Linux and gcc and the compiler tools Lex and YACC
installed.
9. For each laboratory session, create a separate subdirectory, named as "submission" Working programs written
by you along with outputs in the prescribed format are to be saved in this directory. In case there are more than
one problem in the assignment sheet, appropriately name the programs and their outputs suffixed by the problem
number.
LAST, BUT NOT THE LEAST, PLEASE BE ASSURED THAT A STUDENT, WHO USES MEANS / SHORT
CUTS, OTHER THAN WRITING HER / HIS OWN PROGRAMS, WILL BE THE EVENTUAL LOSER.
LACK OF PROGRAMMING SKILLS ARE EASILY DETECTED BY YOUR FACULTY AND YOUR
POTENTIAL EMPLOYERS AND THE CONSEQUENCES ARE OBVIOUS.
